# Results Interpretation

## Topic

The mediating role of negative affect (NA) and the moderating role of positive affect (PA) in the relationship between global executive composite (GEC) and perceived stress


---
## 1. Research questions
In this mini-project, I proposed the following research questions:
### Direct Associations
- Does GEC associate with perceived stress?
- Does GEC associate with NA?
- Does NA associate with perceived stress?
- Does PA associate with GEC?
- Does PA associate with NA?

### Mediation
- Does NA mediate the relationship between GEC and perceived stress?

### Moderated Mediation
- Does PA moderate the indirect effect of GEC on perceived stress via NA?

---

## 2. Material and methods
## Method

### Participants and Data Source

Data were drawn from the transdiagnostic deep-phenotyping neuroimaging dataset described by Samardzija et al. (2025), available via medRxiv. The dataset comprises clinical, behavioral, and resting-state/task fMRI data from over 300 extensively characterized participants. For the present study, we extracted demographic variables (age, sex) and self-report measures of executive function (Behavior Rating Inventory of Executive Function—Global Executive Composite; BRIEF GEC), affect (Positive and Negative Affect Schedule; PANAS), and perceived stress (Perceived Stress Scale; PSS).

### Missing data
To test the type of missing pattern, I used MCAR (missing completely at random) test in SPSS and showed non-significant p-value for the test, indicating that the pattern is MCAR. Additionally, across the entire sample (n=411), 14 participants had missing data and as it is below 5% of the total sample (3.6%), I decided to use listwise deletion. To more info, check (`missing_data.xlsx`)

### Analytic Strategy

Bivariate correlations were first computed to examine zero-order associations. We then conducted multiple linear regression and structural equation modeling (SEM) to test mediation and moderated mediation hypotheses.
